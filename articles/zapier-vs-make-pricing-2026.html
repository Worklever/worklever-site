Zapier vs Make Pricing (2026): Why 44x Cheaper Isn't the Whole Story

Updated 6 August 2026 · every figure read off the vendors' own published pages, retrieval-dated 28 July 2026 · full source ledger below · ~8 min read

Zapier and Make automate the same jobs — connect your form to your spreadsheet, your payments to your invoices — but they charge on completely different logic. One bills only the work that succeeds. The other bills every attempt, including looking at nothing, yet sells its units roughly 44 times cheaper at entry tiers. Here's the actual math, on four real small-business shapes, so you can predict your own bill before connecting a single app.

The verdict up front

Get Make if: you run event-driven automations (forms, orders, payments trigger the work). Its ~$0.0009-per-credit entry tier ($9/mo for 10,000) beat Zapier in three of our four scenarios, twice by about $480/year — and its free tier is 10x bigger.

Get Zapier if: your automations watch constantly and act rarely (scheduled monitors, polling checks). Polling is free on Zapier; on Make every look costs a credit. Our monitor example: Zapier $0, Make $108/year.

Skip both if: you'd automate fewer than ~100 simple events a month — both free tiers cover that, so start free on either and pay only when a real limit bites. And if you're not sure which jobs to automate at all, take our free 2-minute Stack Builder first — picking the workflow matters more than picking the vendor.

The real difference: who pays for watching

They bill on different units, and the difference is not just the trigger — it is who pays for WATCHING. Zapier charges only for successful ACTION steps: the trigger is free, polling is free, and Filters, Paths, Formatter, Delay, Looping and Digest are all free too. Your Zapier bill tracks work COMPLETED. Make charges for every module run including the trigger, and explicitly including every scheduled check that finds nothing at all. Your Make bill tracks work completed PLUS work attempted. Naively that sounds like Make is always more expensive — a 3-action workflow is 3 Zapier tasks but 4 Make credits per run. The opposite is usually true, because the per-unit prices are roughly 44x apart at entry tiers on monthly billing: Make's entry tier is $9 for 10,000 credits (about $0.0009 each), Zapier's is $29.99 for 750 tasks (about $0.040 each; on Zapier's annual rate of $19.99 it's about $0.0267, still roughly 30x). So Make's structural disadvantage on unit COUNT is swamped by its advantage on unit PRICE. The practical rule: Make is cheaper for almost anything that runs on real events at small-business volume, and Zapier wins in exactly one shape — high-frequency polling that rarely finds anything, where Zapier gives the checking away free and Make meters it. Everything else is a coverage, reliability and ease-of-use decision, not a price one.

What each unit actually is

Zapier bills "tasks". "A task is any successful action that runs in Zapier. Only successful actions count toward your task usage." The trigger NEVER counts: "Zap triggers never use tasks. A Zap's first step is always a trigger." Polling for new data is also free. CRITICALLY, these steps do NOT consume tasks either: Filter steps, Paths steps, Formatter, Delay, Looping, Digest, Storage, Zapier Tables and Zapier Forms, plus any action that errors or is skipped by a filter. What DOES count: each successful action step, steps inside sub-Zaps, steps re-run during a full Zap replay, and Search actions set to 'proceed if nothing found' (1 task each; set to 'do not proceed if nothing found' they cost 0).

Make bills "credits". An operation is "a single module run to process data or check for new data," and credits are "the currency you buy and consume to use Make" — "operations count the activities performed in your scenarios, while credits are what you pay for those activities." For standard non-AI modules, 1 operation = 1 credit. THE TRIGGER COUNTS: "Trigger modules use 1 credit per run, regardless of whether they return data" and "even when a trigger finds no new data, it still uses 1 credit for checking." A trigger costs exactly 1 credit per check no matter how many bundles it returns. Action modules are billed per bundle: "action modules (those with 'add,' 'update,' 'delete,' etc) use as many credits as runs needed to process all bundles — 1 credit per input bundle processed," so a step that writes 10 rows costs 10 credits. Iterators cost 1 credit to split an array plus 1 credit per iterated bundle downstream. Make Code costs 2 credits per second of execution. AI actions are token-based and cost more than 1 credit.

Make's model bills every module run, including checks that find nothing. So a Make credit count equals (trigger checks per month) + (billable module runs x bundles). Cost therefore scales with how often you LOOK, not just how often you ACT — the opposite of Zapier. Offsetting that, the published per-unit rate is dramatically lower: Core at $9 for 10,000 credits is about $0.0009 per credit, roughly 44x cheaper per unit than Zapier's monthly-billed $0.040 per task (Zapier's annual rates narrow it to roughly 27-30x). All three paid tiers (Core/Pro/Teams) include the SAME 10,000 base credits — you are paying for features, not volume. Higher volumes (20k, 40k, 80k, 150k... up to 8M+) are selected on a slider; Make does not publish those per-tier prices as static text. Make switched its billing unit name from 'operations' to 'credits' on 27 August 2025, which is why older guides and screenshots say 'operations'.

Four real shapes, priced end-to-end

Same workflow, both platforms, vendors' own published rates (annual billing where shown):

LEAD CAPTURE (2-step, low volume)MAKE WINS

Shape1 trigger + 1 action. Zapier billable steps: 1 (trigger free). Make billable modules: 2 (trigger counts).
Volume400 form submissions
Zapier400 runs x 1 billable action = 400 tasks/month. The Free plan's 2-step shape is fine, but 400 tasks exceeds its 100-task allowance, so this forces Professional at the 750-task rung: $19.99/mo billed annually ($29.99 billed monthly) = ~$240/year.
Make400 runs x 2 modules (trigger + action) = 800 credits/month. That fits inside the Free plan's 1,000 credits, and one scenario is within the 2-active-scenario cap. $0/mo.
VerdictMAKE WINS, ~$240/year. The irony worth noting: Make charges for the trigger and still costs nothing here, purely because its free allowance is 10x Zapier's. Watch the headroom though — Make is at 800 of 1,000 free credits, so roughly 500 submissions/month is the ceiling before Core ($9/mo) is needed.

NEW-CUSTOMER SEQUENCE (3 actions, medium volume)MAKE WINS

Shape1 trigger + 3 actions. Zapier billable steps: 3. Make billable modules: 4.
Volume600 payments
Zapier600 runs x 3 billable actions = 1,800 tasks/month. The 1,500-task rung is not enough, so this lands on the 2,000-task rung: $49.00/mo billed annually ($73.50 billed monthly) = $588/year. Note this is only 200 tasks of headroom.
Make600 runs x 4 modules = 2,400 credits/month. Over the 1,000 free credits, so Core at 10,000 credits: $9/mo = $108/year. Leaves 7,600 credits of headroom.
VerdictMAKE WINS, $480/year. This is the typical small-business shape and it is not close. The step count that hurts Make (4 modules vs 3 tasks) is irrelevant next to the unit price gap. Note also the headroom asymmetry: Zapier is 11% from its ceiling, Make is 76% clear of its.

FILTERED ORDER FLOW (5 steps, high volume, most runs discarded)MAKE WINS*

Shape1 trigger + 1 filter + 4 actions. Zapier billable steps: 4 (trigger AND filter both free). Make billable modules: 1 trigger on every order + 4 actions on the ~25% that pass.
Volume2,000 orders/month, of which ~500 (25%) clear the $500 threshold
ZapierThe filter costs nothing and filtered-out runs bill nothing, so only passing runs count: 500 x 4 actions = 2,000 tasks/month. That is exactly the 2,000-task rung: $49.00/mo billed annually ($73.50 monthly) = $588/year.
MakeThe trigger fires on all 2,000 orders whether they pass or not = 2,000 credits, plus 500 passing runs x 4 action modules = 2,000 credits. Total 4,000 credits/month. Core covers it: $9/mo = $108/year.
VerdictMAKE WINS, $480/year — but this is the clearest illustration of Make's structural weakness: half of Make's 4,000 credits are burned on 1,500 orders that were filtered out and did nothing. If the pass rate dropped to 5%, Zapier's bill would fall to the 750-task rung while Make's trigger cost would not move at all. Assumption flagged: we treated Make's filter as costing 0 credits, which Make's docs do not explicitly confirm.

SCHEDULED MONITOR (polling, acts rarely) — the case where Zapier winsZAPIER WINS

Shape1 trigger + 1 action. Zapier billable steps: 1. Make billable modules: 1 per CHECK plus 1 per action.
Volume2,880 checks/month (every 15 min), of which only ~30 actually trigger an email
ZapierPolling triggers never consume tasks, so the 2,880 checks are free. Only the 30 emails bill: 30 x 1 action = 30 tasks/month. This fits the Free plan on every axis — 30 of 100 tasks, a 2-step Zap, and 15-minute polling is exactly the Free plan's rate. $0/mo.
MakeThe scheduled trigger costs 1 credit per run regardless of whether it returns data: 4 checks/hr x 24 hr x 30 days = 2,880 credits, plus 30 action credits = 2,910 credits/month. That blows past the 1,000 free credits, forcing Core: $9/mo = $108/year.
VerdictZAPIER WINS, $108/year — and it wins from $0, which no amount of Make discounting can beat. This is the one shape where the billing models genuinely invert: 99% of Make's cost here is paying to look at something that had not changed. The rule to remember: the more often you CHECK relative to how often you ACT, the more Zapier's model favours you.

The free tiers, honestly compared

Zapier Free: 100 tasks/month, unlimited Zaps but a hard TWO-STEP limit per Zap (one trigger + one action), 15-minute polling, 1 user. The two-step ceiling is often the binding constraint before the task count is — any workflow needing two actions forces a paid plan regardless of volume.

Make Free: 1,000 credits/month with no time limit, capped at 2 active scenarios and a 15-minute minimum interval for scheduled scenarios. Note this is a 10x larger unit allowance than Zapier's 100 tasks, and Make imposes no step-count limit on a scenario — but every module including the trigger draws on it.

FAQ

What counts as a task in Zapier?

Only a successful action step. Zapier's own docs: "A task is any successful action that runs in Zapier." Triggers never consume tasks, polling is free, and Filter, Paths, Formatter, Delay, Looping and Digest steps are all free too. Your bill tracks work completed.

What counts as a credit in Make?

Every module run — including the trigger, and including scheduled checks that find nothing. One standard operation equals one credit (Make renamed 'operations' to 'credits' in August 2025). Your bill tracks work completed plus work attempted.

Which is cheaper for a typical small business?

Usually Make, and it's rarely close: its entry tier is roughly $9 for 10,000 credits (about $0.0009 per unit) versus Zapier's $29.99/mo billed monthly ($19.99 annually) for 750 tasks (about $0.040 per unit monthly) — around a 44x unit-price gap on monthly billing (roughly 30x on Zapier's annual rate) that outweighs Make billing more units per run. In our worked examples Make won three of four shapes, twice by ~$480/year.

When does Zapier actually win?

When your automation checks constantly but acts rarely — a scheduled monitor polling every 15 minutes that only fires a few times a month. Zapier's free polling makes that shape $0, while Make pays one credit for every look. The rule: the more often you CHECK relative to how often you ACT, the more Zapier's model favours you.
