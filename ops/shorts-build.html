File 1 — name: "Worklever — Short 1: The Empty-Check Meter" (plate: fliki_short_1.png)

Make charges you a credit every time it checks for work — even when there's nothing there. Zapier doesn't. Zapier's own docs say triggers never use tasks, and polling is free — you're billed only when a successful action runs. Same workflow, wildly different unit counts. That doesn't automatically make Make expensive — its units cost about 44 times less at entry tiers. But you can't compare the two headline prices until you know what each one counts.

File 2 — name: "Worklever — Short 2: The 44x Rate" (plate: fliki_short_2.png)

Make's units cost about forty-four times less than Zapier's. That still doesn't tell you which one is cheaper. Here's the maths from the published pages, monthly billing, entry tiers. Zapier Professional: twenty-nine ninety-nine for seven hundred and fifty tasks — four cents a task. Make Core: nine dollars for ten thousand credits — nine hundredths of a cent per credit. But Make counts more events than Zapier: the trigger, every check, every bundle. So the real question is whether Make's higher count outruns its 44-times-cheaper unit. For most workflow shapes we priced — it doesn't.

File 3 — name: "Worklever — Short 3: Free-Plan Ceilings" (plate: fliki_short_3.png)

Both free plans have a ceiling that isn't the number they advertise. Zapier free gives you a hundred tasks a month — but every Zap is capped at two steps: one trigger, one action. So the second you need a filter plus two actions, you're paying. Make free gives you a thousand credits — but only two active scenarios. One limits the shape of your automation, the other limits how many you can run. Check which wall you hit first, because that's what actually sets your bill.
